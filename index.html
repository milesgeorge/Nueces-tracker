Engine. Water elevation at a point = Tilden stage + 183.10 + (slope × river-miles that point sits above the gauge). Depth = that − ground (USGS 3DEP LiDAR). “Slope” starts at 1.36 ft per river-mile (fit to the 2013 ranch marks) and re-fits by weighted least-squares every time you log a sighting. “Water rise above gauge” (offset) = ground + observed depth − 183.10 − gauge reading — the one number the model learns. Mechanism buttons set the crest band from historic analogs (2026 dry-routed, 2018 wet-routed, 2002/2013 local-rain, 1967 Beulah tropical). Custom coordinates carry ±3 ft until a nearby sighting tightens them. Decision aid only — defer to official NWS/NOAA warnings for life-safety.

How to use this model

What this is. The Miles Ranch sits in the ungauged 100-mile reach between the Cotulla and Tilden river gauges, so NOAA forecasts at those gauges but not on your land. This tool bridges that gap: it reads the live gauges and translates them into a predicted water depth and timing at any point on the ranch, calibrated on the confirmed 2013 high-water marks. It is a decision aid — official NWS/NOAA warnings always govern life-safety.

1 · The gauge chain

The row of gauges from the Hill Country down to Three Rivers. Each one upstream is earlier warning — Asherton crests ~8 days before the ranch, Cotulla ~5. The gauges show stage in feet (height on that gauge). The Ranch box has no gauge, so it shows its current modeled status from the live Tilden reading (DRY / RISING / HIGH / FLOODING / FLOODED) — this is “now,” not a forecast. The forecast for a predicted crest lives in Section 3. Gauges auto-update every 15 minutes; the links open the official NOAA pages.

2 · Which kind of flood (mechanism)

The same amount of water behaves very differently depending on how the flood is made. Auto reads the mechanism from the live gauges (mainly the Tilden-vs-Cotulla flow ratio) and picks it for you, explaining why. Tap any button to override for a “what-if” — e.g. force “local rain” or “tropical” to stress-test. A green banner means auto; amber means you’ve overridden, with a link back to auto. Amplifying regimes (local rain, tropical) are the dangerous ones — the ranch can flood even when upstream looks modest.

3 · Forecast & your locations

Slide the Cotulla crest and the mechanism converts it to a predicted Tilden crest (the big red number) — or drag the Tilden slider directly for free what-ifs. Below, the big number is the predicted water-surface elevation at the ranch. The ladder lists every location high-to-low; the blue line is the water surface, rows above it stay dry (green), rows below flood (red) with the depth. Save scenario stores a crest+mechanism you can reload later. ＋ Add location lets you add any spot by coordinates with an auto-fetched ground elevation.

4 · Satellite map

Your locations on aerial imagery, colored green (dry) or red (flooded) for the current scenario. Tap the map anywhere to drop a new location — it grabs the coordinates and fetches the elevation for you.

5 · Reach profile

A side-view of the water surface down the whole chain. Pills between gauges are travel time; ⚠ marks a gauge above flood stage; kcfs is live flow; 🌊 is the wave’s current position.

6 · Teach the model

This is what makes it smarter over time. When someone sees where the water actually is, log it: who, where, the water depth there, and the current Tilden reading. The tool converts that into a measured “water rise above the gauge” and re-fits its slope so every estimate matches reality. New spots you report are saved into your Locations list. Edit or delete any sighting to recalculate. In shared (Firebase) mode, sightings go to an admin for approval before they move the shared model.

7 · Historical context

Every Tilden flood on record since 1935, ranked against your scenario, with a plain-English read of where it sits. ★ marks the five benchmark floods with full detail. Tap any event for its story and a compare/contrast to what you’re watching. After a flood ends and NOAA logs the official crest, press “Update record from NOAA” to pull it in.

Does it refresh itself?

Yes. The gauges auto-pull when you open it and re-pull every 15 minutes while the tab is open, and the forecast, ladder, map and mechanism all recompute in place — no page reload needed. In shared (Firebase) mode, approved sightings appear live for everyone. Your locations, sightings and saved scenarios are stored on your device and persist across reloads (they are not auto-cleared). Use the button below to wipe them.

A note on accuracy

Saved ranch features carry about ±1.2 ft (calibrated on 2013); a brand-new custom coordinate carries about ±3 ft until a nearby sighting tightens it. The forecast assumes no fresh rain falls in the reach — if that changes, switch the mechanism to “local rain” and watch for sightings to confirm.
